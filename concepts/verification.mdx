---
title: "Hash-Chain Verification"
description: "Tamper evidence, not just tamper prevention."
---

Say someone wanted to quietly rewrite history, change an old, already
approved decision to hide what really happened. If the ledger were just
a folder of ordinary files, nothing would stop them, and nothing would
even tell you it happened. Hash-chain verification is what makes that
kind of quiet edit detectable, every time.

## How it works

Every [proposal](/concepts/proposal)'s own ID is a hash computed from its
full content, including a `parent` field pointing at the proposal right
before it. That one detail is what makes the whole chain provable: change
anything inside an old proposal, and its true hash changes too. The
*next* proposal in the chain still points at the *old* hash, since that's
what was true when it was written. The link is now visibly broken.

```bash
ubx verify
```

```bash
verify: 47 proposal(s) checked
chain: intact
```

A clean run recomputes every proposal's hash from scratch, confirms every
`parent` reference matches, and confirms every signature is real.

## Why "nobody would notice" doesn't work

A system that just trusts whatever's on disk, with no way to check it,
relies entirely on nobody ever having a reason to lie, make a mistake, or
get hacked. That's not a real guarantee, it's just an absence of
scrutiny. The moment you actually need to prove something happened a
certain way, "we never checked" isn't an answer anyone can rely on.

## What this actually looks like

If a single old proposal were tampered with, verification doesn't just
fail vaguely, it names exactly where:

```bash
ubx verify
```

```bash
verify: 47 proposal(s) checked
  8224643bbbc5...: hash_mismatch -- recomputed content hash does not match its own id
chain: BROKEN (1 finding(s), see above)
```

To actually hide a change like this, someone would have to recompute and
re-sign every single proposal after the one they edited, all the way to
today, forging every real approval along the way. That's a fundamentally
different, much harder problem than editing one file.

## Why it matters

You don't have to take anyone's word that the history is genuine. You
can check it yourself, at any time, and get a mathematically real
answer, not a guess based on trust.
