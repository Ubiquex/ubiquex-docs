---
title: "The Audit Surface"
description: "why, blame, verify, and stats: how ubx lets you audit itself."
---

Trust in a system that records history isn't worth much if there's no
real way to interrogate that history. A ledger nobody can actually query
is just a very reliable-sounding black box.

## How it works

A small set of commands exist purely to answer honest questions about
your own infrastructure's real history.

```bash
ubx why payments.aws_sqs_queue.pipeline-events
```

Shows a resource's complete history: every proposal that ever touched
it, and who signed each one.

```bash
ubx blame payments.aws_sqs_queue.pipeline-events
```

Per-attribute provenance, not just "this resource changed" but exactly
which proposal set one specific attribute to its current value.

```bash
ubx verify
```

Independent, full-chain verification, covered in its own depth in
[Hash-Chain Verification](/concepts/verification).

## Why "trust the tool" isn't a real answer

A system that asks you to simply believe its own history is real, with
no way to check any of it yourself, is asking for exactly the kind of
blind trust this whole design exists to avoid. A real audit surface
means you never have to just take ubx's word for it.

## What this actually looks like

```bash
ubx stats
```

```bash
signed proposals: 94% of all changes in the last 90 days
median time-to-decision: 4 minutes
```

Real metrics, computed from the ledger itself, never asserted from
outside it. `ubx addresses` rounds this out with a referenceable
inventory of every address currently in the ledger, useful for authoring
a cross-stack reference without guessing at another stack's own resource
names.

## Why it matters

Every real question you'd want to ask about your own infrastructure's
history, who, what, when, has a command that answers it directly from
the ledger, not from a dashboard's own separate interpretation of it.
