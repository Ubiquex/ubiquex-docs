---
title: "The AI Trust Boundary"
description: "AI can draft. It never has execution authority."
---

If an AI is going to help write infrastructure changes, a real question
has to be answered up front: what happens if it gets something wrong, or
makes an assumption you'd never have agreed to? The answer in ubx is
structural, not a policy someone could forget to follow: the AI can
never actually apply anything. It can only draft.

## How it works

When you describe what you want in prose, an AI reads it and produces a
draft, an unsigned proposal, sitting in `.ubx/plans/`. That's the entire
job. Drafting and shipping are two completely separate commands in the
code, and nothing lets a draft skip straight to becoming real.

While drafting, the AI sometimes has to make a judgment call, a default
value it wasn't told, an assumption about what "small" or "standard"
means. None of that stays hidden. Every one of those choices gets
written directly into the receipt you see before shipping.

```bash
ubx plan --from-doc platform.md
```

```bash
AI defaults, you are signing these:
  message_retention_seconds: defaulted to 86400 (1 day),
    not stated in your prompt

Ship payments · +1 create(s)
Only 'yes' accepted:
```

## Why "just trust the AI" doesn't work

A system where an AI could draft and apply a change in one step means
every mistake, every wrong assumption, every hallucinated detail, goes
straight into your real infrastructure with nobody having actually
looked at it. Even a very reliable AI is still wrong sometimes, and
"usually right" isn't a real safety guarantee for something that can
delete a database.

## What this actually looks like

Whether a human wrote the resource definitions directly or an AI drafted
them from a sentence, the exact same confirmation is required before
anything ships:

```bash
ubx ship 45f63ce10849...
```

```bash
Ship payments · +1 create(s)
Only 'yes' accepted: yes
```

The `ship` command has no idea, and doesn't care, whether a human or an
AI produced the draft it's about to apply. The ceremony is identical
either way.

## Why it matters

The AI's real job is turning your intent into a well-formed first draft,
quickly. The decision to actually make something real still belongs
entirely to a person, every single time, with every assumption laid out
plainly first.

This boundary holds even as the AI gets better-informed inputs to draft
from, see [Context-Aware Drafting](/concepts/context-aware-drafting) for
the one, deliberately narrow, read-only exception: it can check a
stack's own name and config before drafting, and nothing else.
