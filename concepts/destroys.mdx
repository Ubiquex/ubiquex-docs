---
title: "Destroys & Tombstones"
description: "Explicit intent, reversed dependency order, and a permanent record."
---

Deleting something is the one kind of infrastructure change you can't
easily undo. A tool that ever inferred a destroy, "you didn't mention
this resource anymore, so I'll remove it", would be one small mistake
away from destroying something real by accident.

## How it works

A destroy is always an explicit, deliberate part of a proposal, never
inferred from what a document simply left out. Resources that depend on
something being destroyed are torn down first, the same dependency graph
that orders creates correctly runs the sequence in reverse for destroys.

Accepting any proposal with a destroy in it requires a real, extra
confirmation:

```bash
ubx terminate payments.aws_sqs_queue.pipeline-events
ubx ship <hash> --confirm-destroys
```

Two separate confirmations are needed: the proposal itself, and the
destroy flag. Neither one alone is enough to remove something real.

## Why "unmentioned means delete" doesn't work

If leaving a resource out of your next document meant ubx should delete
it, one incomplete draft, a section you forgot to paste back in, could
silently destroy real infrastructure with no warning at all. Requiring
every destroy to be named explicitly removes that entire class of
accident.

## What this actually looks like

A destroyed resource's full history stays in the ledger forever, nothing
is erased:

```bash
ubx why payments.aws_sqs_queue.pipeline-events
```

```bash
change 45f63ce1... created
change 8224643b... modified
change c53c22fa... destroyed
```

The destroy proposal is simply the chain's terminal record, the
resource correctly reads as gone from current state, but every step
that led there is still fully visible.

## Why it matters

Destroying something real requires explicit, deliberate intent every
time, with real friction before it can ship, and the record of it never
disappears just because the resource itself is gone.
