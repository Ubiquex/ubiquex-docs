---
title: "Attribution"
description: "Who caused a drift, and when, not just that it happened."
---

Knowing that a resource drifted is useful. Knowing *who* changed it, and
when, is what actually lets a team have a real conversation about
whether it was intentional. Without that, every drift turns into a
guessing game.

## How it works

Where a real audit backend is available, CloudTrail on AWS, Cloud Audit
Logs on GCP, Kubernetes audit logs on a cluster, ubx correlates a drift
against the real events those systems recorded, and names the actual
actor:

```bash
ubx why payments.aws_sqs_queue.pipeline-events
```

```bash
source: cloudtrail
  actor: arn:aws:iam::123456789012:role/platform-admin
  event: PutQueueAttributes, at 2026-08-04T18:02:11Z
```

## Why guessing at who made a change doesn't work

Without real correlation against an actual audit log, "who did this"
would have to be inferred, from a timestamp, a hunch, someone's
memory of who was working that day. That's not evidence, it's a guess
dressed up as an answer, and it's exactly the kind of thing that erodes
trust in an audit trail the moment it turns out to be wrong.

## What this actually looks like

Attribution can genuinely fail, an account isn't logging, delivery
hasn't caught up yet, or no matching event exists at all. When it does,
that failure is recorded as its own honest, specific reason, never
silently dropped:

```bash
source: cloudtrail_unattributed
  reason: no_matching_event
```

And if more than one real event could plausibly explain a drift, all of
them are recorded, ubx never quietly picks one on your behalf.

## Why it matters

Attribution turns "something changed" into "this specific person, at
this specific time, made this specific change," wherever the real
evidence supports that claim, and says so honestly whenever it doesn't.
