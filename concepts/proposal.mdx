---
title: "The Proposal"
description: "The signed, hashed unit of change ubx records."
---

Every entry in [the ledger](/concepts/ledger) needs a real, fixed shape,
otherwise "reading back history" would mean parsing whatever loose
structure each tool, or each person, happened to write. A proposal is
that fixed shape: one JSON document, the same fields every time,
representing exactly one real decision.

## How it works

A proposal answers a handful of specific questions, always in the same
place: what changed (`delta`), why (`intent`), what it was checked
against (`resolution`), how expensive it is (`cost_delta` and
`blast_radius`), and, once someone approves it, who signed off and how
(`acceptance`).

A proposal's own identity, its `id`, isn't a number you or ubx picks. It's
the SHA-256 hash of the proposal's own content. That has a real,
practical consequence: two people who independently write the exact same
change end up with the exact same ID, because the ID is entirely
determined by what's actually in the file.

```json
{
  "id": "45f63ce10849...",
  "kind": "change",
  "parent": "8224643bbbc5...",
  "delta": { "creates": [ "..." ] },
  "blast_radius": { "creates": 1, "modifies": 0, "destroys": 0 },
  "status": "accepted"
}
```

`parent` points at the hash of the proposal right before this one. That
single field is what turns a folder full of separate files into one
real, ordered chain (more on why that matters in
[Hash-Chain Verification](/concepts/verification)).

## Why a loosely-shaped record doesn't work

Imagine instead that each change was just recorded as free-form notes,
"bumped the queue's retention to 14 days, seemed fine." That's readable
to a human right after the change, but it can't be checked by a machine,
can't be hashed reliably (the same change described two different ways
would produce two different hashes), and has no fixed place to record
things like cost or blast radius consistently.

A proposal's fixed shape means every tool, every command, every future
version of ubx, can read any proposal ever written and know exactly
where to find what it needs.

## What this actually looks like

Accepting a hand-written or resolved proposal is a real command, not
just a description:

```bash
ubx accept proposal.json --ledger-dir .
```

```bash
accepted 45f63ce10849... (stack payments)
```

That single line means the proposal's hash was computed, its
`acceptance` field was filled in with a real signature, and it was
appended to the ledger, permanently.

## Why it matters

Because the shape is fixed and the ID is computed, not chosen, you can
independently verify a proposal's own integrity without trusting
whoever wrote it. Two proposals with the same content always produce
the same hash, and any proposal whose content changes even slightly
produces a completely different one. Nothing about "what actually
happened" depends on anyone's word alone.
