---
title: "Chat Medium: Depth"
description: "Dialogue capture, redaction at the moment of typing, and contradiction handling."
---

A real conversation about what infrastructure you want often isn't
linear, you say one thing, then reconsider and say something different
a few messages later. A tool that only captured the final message would
lose the reasoning behind the final decision. One that captured
everything but couldn't handle a genuine change of mind would be
confusing to review later.

## How it works

The chat medium is the same AI drafting as markdown, held as a real,
ongoing conversation instead of one static document:

```bash
ubx chat
> We need a Postgres database for payments, like staging but smaller.
> Make it multi-az.
```

Every turn is captured and redacted the moment you type it, not as a
later pass over raw text. Unlike a document, which exists independently
before ubx ever reads it, a typed turn has no separate raw original, the
redacted text you see captured is the authoritative record.

## Why capturing only the final message doesn't work

If only the last message mattered, a reviewer looking back at why a
decision was made would lose the actual reasoning, the back-and-forth
that led there. Keeping the whole conversation, redacted as it's typed,
means the full context is available later, not just the final outcome.

## What this actually looks like

If a later message contradicts an earlier one:

```bash
> instance class db.t3.large
> actually, use db.t3.micro instead, not large
```

The final draft reflects the later turn, and the change is recorded as
an explicit assumption naming both turns and which one won, nothing is
silently overwritten or lost.

## Why it matters

The full conversation, and the resolved draft it produced, both become
part of the signed proposal once you ship. Anyone reviewing it later
sees the real reasoning, not just the final answer.
